Welcome to the start of your ride. The calm water beside you is Lost Lagoon. Take a moment to look across it, because its name comes from a poem. The poet Pauline Johnson, also known as Tekahionwake, wrote a poem called "The Lost Lagoon," and it dates to 1910. Her words gave this water its name. Twelve years later, in 1922, the Park Board made it official and adopted the name Lost Lagoon. So every time someone says those two words, they are quoting a poet, whether they know it or not. It's a nice thought to carry with you as you ride: that a place can be named not by a survey or a committee, but by the title of a poem. When you're ready, follow the route toward the water's edge. Next, we'll meet the seawall, and the stonemason who spent decades building it by hand.
