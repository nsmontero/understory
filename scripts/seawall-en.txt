You're now riding on one of the best-loved paths in the city: the Stanley Park seawall. It's easy to take it for granted. It feels as if it has always been here. But it hasn't. Work on the seawall began in 1917, and it wasn't finished until 1971. That's more than fifty years of building. Much of the early work was led by a Scottish stonemason named James Cunningham. He spent decades building sections of this wall by hand, stone by stone. Think about that as your wheels roll along. The wall beside you was set in place slowly, by people, over a lifetime of work. The next time you pass a stretch of carefully fitted stone, you might give a small nod to James Cunningham. Keep the water on your right and ride on. Our next stop is an arch built as a tribute to the logging industry: Lumbermen's Arch.
